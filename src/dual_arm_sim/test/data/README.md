# Block surface regression captures

`red3_before_pick.pcd` and `red3_after_drop.pcd` are binary-compressed XYZRGB clouds of the same simulated 50 mm block, captured from `SendPointCloudToUI` before picking and after a dropped grasp. Coordinates are in the fixed world/model frame, in metres; the PCD format does not preserve ROS frame or timestamp headers. The test supplies its own header and verifies its preservation.

The block's independently measured MuJoCo top is at world z = 0.804362824 m in both captures. Before picking, the mask includes the top and sides (1,925 points). After dropping, the block has moved from (0.8500, -0.2500) m to (0.7338, -0.2575) m and rests on a different face with yaw; the mask contains 1,825 points including 32 floor points. Whole-cloud centroids are 7.99 mm and 20.13 mm below the top. These captures cover this displacement and face change, not arbitrary tilted or merged objects.

`red3_on_floor.pcd` captures a later dropped block at world centre (0.64484, -0.78819, 0.02497) m. Only 55% of its segmented cloud belongs to the largest plane, below the configured 60% acceptance fraction. The test verifies that this rejected surface is an error, not empty detections.

`test_block_surface.cpp` replays the clouds through the shipped Find subtree with only inference and marker publication replaced. Point selection, plane extraction and centroid computation use the installed Behaviors. All geometry and colour data are synthetic Simulator output; no camera images, credentials or operational logs are included.

`right_arm_carry.json` and `right_arm_far_block_carry.json` hold recorded pregrasp joint states and measured position samples of the arm/spine actuators during a 100 mm lift followed by a carry to the right table, for the near and far red blocks. Sample times are elapsed simulation seconds; joint positions are radians or metres according to the model joint type. The offline test uses these samples as actuator position references, not as kinematic constraints: it sets the initial state once, then steps the complete articulated scene, including both fingers and free blocks. The gripper closes after 0.45 s and stays commanded closed; the trajectory is replayed at the Objective's quarter-speed carry setting. This is a deterministic contact regression that reproduces a block yawing out during carry; it does not replay the live controller's internal references or guarantee hardware performance.

`block_transfer_states.json` contains seven recorded pre-pickup states: all six default-scene pickups in sequence, including previously placed blocks, and a second red-block pickup from a different joint configuration. `qpos` follows the compiled scene's joint order; free-body quaternions and approach orientations are `wxyz`. Each approach is the measured surface pose plus the existing 75 mm standoff. The active hand is opened to 40 mm per finger to match the Objective's preplanning step. Joint angles are radians and distances are metres; there are no timestamps, camera images or logs.

`test_block_transfer.py` derives robot parameters and Place waypoints from the current config. Its C++ companion runs the shipped `Plan Block Transfer` with installed planners and solution splitting, substituting only scene and waypoint acquisition. The test checks that the two solutions join at the grasp, that lift precedes carry, and that every post-lift sample clears the other recorded blocks and tables with a cube bounding sphere plus 25 mm for measured within-grip movement. Initial lift begins in intended table support contact. An unreachable target must fail without executable solutions. These are planning regressions, not a replacement for the live contact and end-to-end tests.
